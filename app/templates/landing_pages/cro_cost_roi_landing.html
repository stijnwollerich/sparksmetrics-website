What a CRO agency does

A CRO agency studies how people move through your store or funnel—where they hesitate, misunderstand the offer, or leave. The work is a loop: research, clear hypotheses, controlled experiments, shipped improvements, and measurement so you learn what actually changed behavior.

Quick answers

Red flags when hiring a CRO agency? expand_more
Vague “strategy only” retainers with no test calendar, no clear ownership of dev/QA, or promises that never show up in writing. Strong shops spell out velocity, tooling, and how wins are measured before you sign.
How long until CRO usually pays off? expand_more
Most programs need a few shipping cycles before you can read lift with confidence—think weeks of setup, then a steady drumbeat of tests. If someone promises overnight revenue jumps with no traffic or no tests, treat that as a warning sign.
Is a CRO retainer worth it? expand_more
It tends to matter when you already have meaningful traffic and margin: small conversion gains compound across every session. If you are still proving product–market fit or have almost no volume, you may get more leverage from creative and acquisition first.
What should I know about “ROI guarantees”? expand_more
Some agencies advertise ROI-back or multiplier terms; almost all narrow who qualifies, how uplift is counted, and over what window. Treat marketing language as a prompt to read the contract, not as a substitute for one.
How do agencies usually price CRO? expand_more
Monthly retainers are common because the work is ongoing—research, experiments, fixes, reporting. A few shops blend in performance elements; either way, ask what is included at each fee level and what happens if priorities shift mid-quarter.
What should I ask before I sign? expand_more
Ask for a sample test plan, who touches your theme or checkout, how reporting is delivered, and how they handle losing tests. Good answers are specific; hand-waving about “best practices” without a calendar rarely ages well.

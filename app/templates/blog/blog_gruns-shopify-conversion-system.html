Shopify homepage optimization: why Grüns removed the menu

Most Shopify stores treat the homepage like a brochure: navigation, categories, content, and a bunch of “learn more” exits.

Grüns does the opposite. They've basically turned the homepage into a decision page. No classic menu. No browsing rabbit holes. Two primary paths (Adults and Kids) and a very direct flow into purchase.

This is a strong Shopify conversion rate optimization move when you sell a small product set, because navigation is not just “helpful UX.” It is also an escape hatch for hesitation. If your core goal is subscription sign-ups, reducing choices often improves conversion because the next step stays obvious.

When this Shopify homepage move works (and when it won't)

Supplements aren't like fashion. People rarely want to browse 90 SKUs for fun. They want a result, and they want to feel safe buying. If you have 1–3 hero products, a simplified homepage can outperform a traditional storefront. If you have a large catalog with lots of discovery shopping, removing navigation can backfire.

Shopify hero section best practices: social proof + problem/solution messaging

The hero section is where Grüns flexes, and they do it in the order that actually matters.

First: proof. They hit you with big trust signals immediately (ratings/reviews and a “million members” style claim). This reduces the mental load of asking, “Is this legit?” before you even process the headline.

Then: a simple direct-response structure.

“You have nutrition gaps. Grüns fills them.”

Problem. Solution. Clean. And then a supporting paragraph that explains how they can make a claim that bold.

Shopify CTA optimization: turning the main button into a value statement

Their primary CTA isn't “Buy now.” It communicates value (savings and free shipping). That matters because people do not click buttons because of verbs. They click because they expect an outcome.

Right below the CTA, they add risk reversal (a 30-day guarantee). This is not decoration. It's placed at the exact moment someone asks, “What if I waste my money?” One small line can remove a huge mental tax.

Product page optimization on Shopify: why images often convert better than copy

Grüns understands a painful truth: most shoppers will scroll images before they read paragraphs. So their image carousel isn't just product beauty shots. It's a sales deck.

They use the carousel to communicate what the product is, what to expect, key stats, and benefit highlights. That approach works especially well on mobile, where reading long sections feels like homework.

Shopify product images: text in images without losing SEO

If you use text inside images, do two things so you don't become invisible to search:

  • Use descriptive alt text on every screenshot and image (example: “Grüns mobile subscription selector showing cost-per-day pricing”).
  • Mirror the important claims in actual HTML copy nearby so Google can index it and users who skim still catch the value.

How to increase subscription conversion on Shopify: the offer selector is the engine

This is where the real money is.

Grüns doesn't just ask “how many bags.” They build an offer selector that makes the choice feel personal, then makes the subscription feel safe.

They segment choices early (low sugar vs sugar-free), then flavor. When you change flavor, the page changes visually. That sounds small, but it creates emotional reinforcement: you're not picking from a spreadsheet, you're choosing something that feels like yours.

Bundle pricing and AOV: 1 person vs 2 people (and why cost-per-day wins)

Next comes the upsell: one person or two people. This is classic bundle strategy. They show big discount percentages, the total price, and then the move that matters most: cost per day.

$90 is expensive.

$1.61 per day feels like a habit.

That reframing reduces price pain and increases perceived affordability. It's one of the cleanest ways to increase average order value (AOV) in consumables.

Right next to the subscription decision, they place the objections and the answers:

  • Fast and free shipping
  • Pause or cancel anytime
  • 30-day money-back guarantee

If you want higher subscription conversion, don't hide this in a FAQ. Put it next to the choice.

One-time vs subscription on Shopify: how Grüns makes the smart choice obvious

They still offer one-time purchase, but the value stack is clearly worse (less savings, worse shipping value, higher cost per day). That's not manipulation. It's merchandising. The subscription is positioned as the default rational decision.

Shopify social proof strategy: video testimonials and proof navigation

Anyone can write testimonials. Video is harder, and shoppers know it.

Grüns uses video proof, then categorizes it by intent (benefits, ingredients, experience). That matters because different shoppers have different questions, and the faster someone finds the proof that matches their concern, the faster they move.

If you want implementation routes on Shopify, these are the tools teams typically use:

  • Reviews/UGC: Loox, Judge.me, Yotpo
  • Subscriptions: Recharge
  • Upsells: Rebuy, Zipify, and “Frequently Bought Together” style widgets

You don't need all of them. You need the behavior.

Shopify comparison tables: how to stop competitor Googling

A shopper's brain wants to ask, “Is there something like this that's cheaper?” If you don't answer it, they'll leave your funnel to answer it themselves.

Grüns solves that with a comparison table that frames the market inside their page. Yes, it's biased. But it's effective because it reduces bounce rate and satisfies the urge to compare without opening a new tab.

Shopify kids funnel: conversion copy for parents and AOV upsell

Even when Grüns sells to kids, the copy is written for parents, because parents are the buyer.

They use “usage occasions” to make the product feel like a habit (lunchbox, after school, morning routine). That's not fluff. It's a mental installation process: the buyer can picture exactly when this becomes part of life.

Then the upsell question isn't “how many packs?” It's “how many kids?” That changes the math emotionally. If you have 3 kids, buying 1 feels unfair, so bundles feel natural.

They also cross-sell the adult product in the kids flow with a simple behavioral line: kids stick to it more when you do too. It educates, sells, and makes the parent the hero.

Shopify cart optimization: coupon curiosity as a quantity upsell

In cart, they use a tactic that's both sneaky and clean: “coupon curiosity.” A button that looks like it applies savings also nudges quantity (and then reinforces the discount logic).

It works because “apply coupon” feels helpful, not salesy, and it avoids the friction of a pushy upsell modal. Next to the checkout decision, they repeat proof and guarantees, because the click point is where reassurance is most valuable.

Shopify Plus checkout customization: trust stacking with Checkout Extensibility

Checkout is where buyers get cold feet. Grüns treats it like the last mile of persuasion.

They stack trust signals (customers, shipping speed, guarantee) and repeat savings again. On standard Shopify plans, checkout customization is limited. On Shopify Plus, you get more flexibility, especially with Checkout Extensibility patterns, to add reassurance blocks and upsells without turning checkout into a Frankenstein.

Grüns looks like the kind of brand that pays for that control, and they use it to keep the buyer thinking: “I'm safe. Everyone buys this. I'm getting a deal.”

Shopify mobile CRO: why this funnel works for Meta and TikTok traffic

Supplements scale through paid social, and paid social lands on mobile. If you're copying Grüns, audit mobile first:

  • Thumb-zone reachability: can you tap key choices one-handed?
  • Offer selector UX: does choosing flavor and bundle feel effortless?
  • Sticky CTAs: do they help, or do they cover the value stack?
  • Speed and perceived speed: do images load fast enough to keep momentum?

If you run Meta or TikTok ads, your mobile PDP is your real homepage.

Shopify CRO takeaways: what to steal from Grüns

  1. Simplify navigation if you sell a small product set.
  2. Segment personas immediately (Adults vs Kids).
  3. Put massive social proof above the headline.
  4. Turn CTAs into value statements.
  5. Increase subscription conversion with per-day framing and easy cancel language.
  6. Use image carousels to educate quickly (especially on mobile).
  7. Add proof in snackable formats (big numbers, short explanations).
  8. Use comparison tables to reduce bounce rate and competitor research.
  9. Put reassurance at the exact click points: cart and checkout.

FAQ: Grüns funnel, Shopify CRO, and subscription optimization

Why does removing navigation improve Shopify conversion rate?

For single-product or small-catalog stores, navigation creates exits. Removing it increases clarity, reduces indecision, and keeps the shopper moving forward.

How does Grüns increase subscription conversion?

They reduce subscription fear (pause/cancel anytime, guarantees), make the subscription the best value, and reframe the price as a daily habit cost.

What is cost-per-day pricing and why does it work?

It reframes a large purchase into a small routine expense. It lowers price pain and increases perceived affordability, especially for consumables.

Do comparison tables help Shopify conversion rate?

Yes, when done well. They satisfy the shopper's need to compare without leaving your funnel, and they let you control the framing.

What Shopify apps can help replicate this system?

Review tools (Loox/Judge.me/Yotpo), subscription tools (Recharge), and upsell tools (Rebuy/Zipify) can help. But the strategy matters more than the tool.
